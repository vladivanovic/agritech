## 1. Wild boar （猪・イノシシ) — the primary threat

### 1.1 Scale of the problem

- Boar damage in **Fukuoka Prefecture alone was ~¥330 million in FY2020** (peak ¥582M in FY2010); the prefecture's target is to hold it below ¥250M by FY2026. This is a serious, standing pressure on Munakata orchards, not an edge case.
- Active year-round; damage peaks Mar–May (spring foraging) and Sep–Nov (autumn fattening) — note the autumn peak coincides with mikan harvest.
- Attracted by fallen fruit, chestnuts, persimmons, corn and vegetables; works field edges and overgrown margins at night; a single night's incursion can destroy 10–20% of a small orchard's crop or damage young trees badly.
- **Sanitation is prevention:** pick up fallen fruit promptly — a fruit grower's own drops are the bait.

### 1.2 Fencing — the measure that works

Install **before planting**. Standard prefectural guidance design:

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Wire mesh fence | ~1.0–1.5 m high, posts every 2–3 m |
| Buried skirt | Mesh buried ~30 cm or laid outward at the base — boar dig |
| Outward overhang / angled top | Discourages climbing/pushing over |
| Electric wire | 2–4 strands at ~20–30 cm intervals up the outside face, properly energised and earthed |

Indicative costs (get a local quote; fencing is usually priced per metre installed):

| Option | Indicative cost |
|---|---|
| Wire mesh 1.5 m | ~¥3,000–5,000 per metre |
| Mesh + electric combined | ~¥4,500–7,000 per metre |
| Stand-alone electric fence kit (100 m) | ¥80,000–150,000 |
| Solar energiser (off-grid sites) | ¥30,000–50,000 |

For this project (~260 m perimeter around 0.4 ha) the finance model budgets **¥550,000** for a mesh-plus-electric installation — an estimate to be replaced by a quote. Some municipalities subsidise fence installation for farmers; ask the city/機構 when planning.

### 1.3 Supplementary deterrents (secondary value)

Boar habituate to anything constant. These supplement fencing; they do not replace it:

- Motion-sensor lights along the perimeter (¥8,000–20,000 each).
- Noise devices (gas guns, rotating sound sources) — check local noise expectations with neighbours; rotate or move them regularly.
- Scent repellents — short-lived (weeks), rotate products.

### 1.4 Capture — how it actually works

- **The money flows the other way from the original draft.** Municipalities pay **capture bounties/incentives to licensed trappers**; a farmer does not hire a hunter at "¥5,000–10,000 per boar" (that figure, and the direction of payment, were wrong).
- Trapping requires a **hunting licence （狩猟免許； for traps, わな猟免許）** and capture permission under the Wildlife Protection and Hunting Act framework; in practice, farmers work through the local hunters' association （猟友会） or the municipality's pest-control programme rather than trapping personally.
- Report recurring damage to Munakata City （農林水産課 / 有害鳥獣担当） — damage reports drive the local capture programme.

## 2. Other animals

### 2.1 Deer （シカ）

- Bark-rubbing and browsing damage young trees.
- Prevention: individual tree guards (~¥500–1,000/tree) for young stock; fencing must be **taller than for boar — ~1.8 m** — if deer pressure is the design case (decide which animal you are fencing against before building).

### 2.2 Japanese macaque （サル）

- Fruit raiding, more likely near mountain edges in Munakata.
- Prevention: the same fencing base, plus netting over high-value blocks; roofed enclosures (¥200,000–500,000 extra) are a last resort for severe pressure.

### 2.3 Birds (crows, bulbuls)

- Peck ripening fruit — mainly a peach/pear problem at harvest.
- Prevention: bird netting over blocks during ripening (~¥15,000–30,000 per 100 m²), reflective scare tape (¥3,000–5,000 per 50 m), prompt harvest.

## 4. Priority order

1. **Fence first, before planting** — mesh + electric, buried skirt.
2. Fallen-fruit sanitation, every week of the season.
3. Bird netting on ripening peach/pear.
4. Lights/deterrents as supplements.
5. If damage occurs despite the above: report to the city and work through the 猟友会 / municipal capture programme.
