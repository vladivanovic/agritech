# Field Guide: Onions

## 1. Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆ (Moderate-High for Munakata, Fukuoka)
- **Optimal temperature**: 13-24°C; bulbing triggered when day length exceeds 12-14 hours
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 7a-9b (Munakata Zone 9b-10a - excellent)
- **Growing season**: Plant Oct-Nov, harvest May-June (spring-summer crop)
- **Rainfall**: 1,000-1,500mm adequately; good drainage essential (onions rot in wet soil)

## 2. Planting

### 2.1 Seed/Set Preparation
- **Sets (recommended for beginners)**: Small dormant bulbs, 1-2cm diameter
- **Seed**: Direct sow or start indoors 6-8 weeks before transplanting
- **Preparation**: Separate sets, discard any soft or damaged bulbs

### 2.2 Field Preparation
- **Soil type**: Well-draining loam or sandy loam; pH 6.0-6.8 optimal
- **Bed preparation**: Raised beds 20-30cm high, 60cm wide, any length
- **Spacing**: 
  - Sets: 10-15cm between plants, 30cm between rows
  - Seed: 1cm deep, 5cm apart, thin to 10-15cm
- **Fertilizer**: Base fertilizer - organic compost 5-10kg/m²; 
  - Top-dressing: Nitrogen 30-50g/m² at planting, side-dress 30 days after planting

### 2.3 Planting Procedure
1. **Bed formation**: Raise beds, lay black plastic mulch (optional, warms soil, suppresses weeds)
2. **Planting**: Sets 2-3cm deep, pointed end up; space 10-15cm apart
3. **Watering**: Thorough watering after planting (30L/m²); maintain consistent moisture
4. **Mulching**: Straw or dry grass 5cm to conserve moisture, suppress weeds

## 3. Maintenance

### 3.1 Weed Control
- **Hand weeding**: Required until canopy closes (6-8 weeks)
- **Mulch**: Black plastic or straw suppresses weeds
- **Hoeing**: Light cultivation between rows if no mulch

### 3.2 Fertilization Schedule
- **Base** (bed prep): Organic compost 5-10kg/m² + balanced fertilizer (N-P-K 8-8-8) 100g/m²
- **Side-dress 1** (3 weeks after planting): Nitrogen 30g/m² to promote leaf growth
- **Side-dress 2** (bulbing stage, ~6 weeks): Potassium 50g/m² (supports bulb development); reduce nitrogen

### 3.3 Thinning & Spacing
- If direct-seeded, thin seedlings to 10-15cm apart when 10-15cm tall
- Proper spacing ensures bulb development and air circulation

## 4. Water Requirements

### 4.1 Irrigation Schedule
- **Total water requirement**: 500-700mm throughout growing season
- **Weekly requirement**: 20-30mm water (equivalent to 200-300L/m²) during active growth
- **Reduce water**: 2-3 weeks before harvest (stop irrigation 10-14 days before) to prevent bulb rot

### 3.2 Moisture Management
- **Critical periods**: 
  - Planting to 6 weeks: Consistent moisture for establishment
  - Bulbing stage (6-10 weeks): Slightly drier; avoid waterlogged soil
  - 2-3 weeks before harvest: Withhold irrigation to trigger maturity and prevent rot
- **Method**: Drip irrigation recommended; overhead irrigation acceptable if foliage kept dry (prevents disease)

### 3.3 Signs of Water Stress
- **Too much water**: Yellowing leaves, soft bulbs, fungal growth, split bulbs
- **Too little water**: Stunted growth, small bulbs, premature bolting, yellowing leaf tips

## 5. Pest & Disease Management

### 5.1 Common Pests
| Pest | Damage | Control |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Onion fly/maggot**: Larvae tunnel into bulbs; yellowing, stunted growth | Yellow sticky traps, nematode seed treatment, row covers |
| **Thrips**: Silver streaks on leaves, reduced growth; vector for viruses | Blue sticky traps, neem oil spray, beneficial insects (predatory mites) |
| **Aphids**: Cluster on leaves, suck sap; transmit viruses | Insecticidal soap, neem oil, beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings) |
| **Onion thrips**: Tiny, dark; scrape leaf surface | Same as thrips; remove weeds (alternate hosts) |

### 5.2 Common Diseases
| Disease | Symptoms | Control |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Downy mildew**: Yellow patches on leaves, white downy growth underside; worse in cool, wet | Bordeaux mixture (copper fungicide), improve air circulation, avoid overhead watering |
| **Purple blotch**: Purple-brown spots on leaves; spreads quickly in warm, humid | Copper fungicides, crop rotation (3+ years), remove infected debris |
| **Botrytis neck rot**: Soft rot at neck of bulb; storage rot | Cure bulbs properly (dry 2-3 weeks), store in cool dry place, remove diseased bulbs |
| **White rot**: Basal rot, white fungal growth on bulb, sclerotia (black hard bodies) | No effective chemical control; rotate crops 5+ years, sanitize equipment |

### 5.3 Protection Methods
- **Row covers**: Floating row covers (reduction of pest pressure, 70-80% exclusion)
- **Crop rotation**: 3+ years before planting alliums in same spot
- **Sanitation**: Remove cull bulbs, infected plant material, rotate equipment
- **Resistant varieties**: Some varieties have tolerance to specific diseases

## 6. Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: 
  - Neck (top of bulb) softens and falls over
  - Leaves turn yellow/brown (bottom leaves first)
  - Bulb skin papery, outer skin dry
- **Harvest timing**: 
  - Spring-sown: May-June harvest
  - Fall-planted (sets): April-May harvest second year, or late summer first year
- **Harvest method**: 
- **Cure**: 2-3 weeks in warm, dry, well-ventilated area (25-30°C, 60-70% humidity)
- **Storage**: 
  - 3-6 months at 0-5°C, 60-70% humidity
  - Keep away from apples/pears (ethylene gas promotes sprouting)
  - Check regularly, remove any rotting bulbs
- **Yield**: 
  - 3-6kg per m² for mature bulbs
  - 1-2kg per m² for green onions/scallions (earlier harvest)

## 6. Field Layout & Rotation
- **Rotation**: 3+ years before replanting onions/alliums in same field
- **Good followers**: Leafy greens, legumes, corn, solanaceous crops (tomato, pepper)
- **Avoid following**: Other alliums (garlic, leeks), strawberries, potatoes (shared disease risk)
- **Intercropping**: Carrots, lettuce, spinach can be between onion rows early in season
