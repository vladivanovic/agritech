## Executive Summary

This guide compiles the legal, financial, and practical requirements for a foreign national to transition to agricultural land ownership and production in Munakata, Fukuoka. It covers permits, property acquisition, equipment costs, and local resources. Research indicates significant barriers for foreigners but viable pathways through municipal programs and strategic partnerships.

**Recommended Approach:**
1. Try diverse crops: citrus, strawberries, grapes, peaches, pears
2. Seasonal risk diversification
3. Partner with local agricultural cooperatives

## 6. Diverse Fruit Cultivation Guide

### 6.1 Mikan (Mandarins - Citrus)

**Climate Suitability**: ★★★★★ Best

#### Recommended Varieties

| Variety | Characteristics | Harvest Season |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Harumikan | Early spring, high quality | Mar-Apr |
| Kiyomi | Large fruit, less acidity | Dec-Jan |
| Asami | High sugar content, juicy | Feb-Mar |

#### Grafting Techniques

**Bud Grafting (Summer, June-August)**
- Success rate: 70-85%
- Procedure:
  1. Cut scion at ground level, 10cm high, horizontal
  2. Make 1cm deep vertical incision on rootstock
  3. Cut scion at 45-degree angle
  4. Insert scion into rootstock incision
  5. Precisely align cambium layers
  6. Fix with 2-3 rings of tape
  7. Manage for 4 weeks

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. **Preparation**:
   - Healthy scion (1-year growth)
   - Select healthy rootstock
   - Sharpen grafting knife
   - Disinfect with 75% alcohol

2. **Procedure**:
   - Cut rootstock at 10cm, create horizontal surface
   - Cut scion at 45-degree angle
   - Insert scion into rootstock incision
   - Precisely align cambium
   - Fix with ring tape

3. **Aftercare**:
   - Remove rings after 3-4 weeks
   - Prune competing buds
   - Spray pesticide every 2 weeks for 2 months

### Points to Watch Out for in Greenhouse Cultivation

1. **Temperature Control**
   - Summer temperature rise: Ventilation essential to prevent exceeding 30°C during day
   - Winter cold: Maintain minimum 5°C, use heaters at night
   - Condensation control: Mold prevention measures

2. **Humidity Management**
   - Optimal humidity: 60-80%
   - Mold prevention during high humidity: Avoid leaf watering, manage soil moisture
   - Low humidity measures: Humidifier or mist spraying

3. **Pests and Diseases**
   - Risk in enclosed environments: Early detection and immediate elimination
   - Common pests: Aphids, mites, Whitefly
   - Common diseases: Powdery mildew, gray mold, root rot

4. **Ventilation Management**
   - Natural ventilation: Adjust side and top openings
   - Forced ventilation: Fan-assisted air circulation
   - CO2 concentration: Optimal 400-800 ppm

5. **Water Management**
   - Watch for both over-wet and dry conditions
   - Morning watering: Minimize evaporation loss
   - Soil moisture meter recommended for measurement

6. **Nutrient Management**
   - Fertilization frequency: Adjust according to growth stage
   - Top-dressing based on soil test results
   - Balance between organic and chemical fertilizers

7. **Notes on Pest and Disease Control**
   - Disinfect shoe soles on entry
   - Disinfect tools after use
   - Immediately remove abnormal plants

8. **Electricity Cost Management**
   - Cost of ventilation fans and heaters
   - Suggestion to use solar power
   - Operation considering time-of-use pricing

#### Bud Grafting Procedure for Self-Increase

**Best Season**: June-August (Summer)

**Success Rate**: 70-85% (Up to 90% at expert level)

**Procedure**:

1. **Rootstock Selection and Preparation**
   - Use 1-year-old healthy seedling as rootstock
   - Height: Cut at ground level, 10-15cm high
   - Select seedling with diameter 1-2cm
   - Disinfect with alcohol

2. **Scion Preparation**
   - Select healthy branch from next year's growth
   - Length: 10-15cm segments
   - Select 2-3 healthy buds
   - Remove lower leaves
   - Disinfect with alcohol

3. **Cutting Procedure**
   - Rootstock: Make 1cm deep vertical incision
   - Scion: Cut at 45-degree angle (forming callus)
   - Precisely align cambium layers
   - Wrap with ring tape 2-3 times

4. **Fixing and Protection**
   - Wrap tightly with tape, covering all gaps
   - Cover with protective sponge to prevent drying and injury
   - Manage in semi-shade, avoid direct sunlight

5. **Management and Growth**
   - Check connection after 3-4 weeks (bud swelling)
   - Remove ring (peel tape) if needed
   - Pesticide spray once a week (fungicide/insecticide)
   - After 2 months: ready for transplanting

#### Chip Budding Procedure for Self-Increase

**Best Season**: May-July (Spring to Early Summer)

**Success Rate**: 65-80%

**Procedure Overview**:
1. Select healthy location on rootstock with diameter 2-3cm
2. Make vertical incision on rootstock (10cm × 0.5cm)
3. Create "shield" shape on scion
4. Insert shield into rootstock incision
5. Wrap with binding tape 1-2 rotations
6. Protect exposed parts with grafting wax
7. Manage in shade, check connection after 1 month

#### Cleft Grafting Procedure for Self-Increase

**Best Season**: February-March (Winter to Early Spring)

**Success Rate**: 75-85%

**Procedure Overview**:
1. Cut rootstock in V-shape
2. Insert scion halfway into incision
3. Fix with ring tape
4. Seal with 45-degree angle
5. Check connection after 3-4 weeks of management

|-----------------|--------|---------|

| Ring Removal | 4-6 weeks after | Tape removal, connection check |

| Pruning | After 1 year | Shaping and unwanted shoot removal |

### Key Points for Vegetable Cultivation

1. **Leafy Vegetables (Spinach, komatsuna, etc.)**
   - Optimal cultivation in cold season (Nov-Mar)
   - Condensation control: mold prevention
   - Morning watering and check evening condition

2. **Fruit Vegetables (Tomatoes, cucumbers, eggplants, etc.)**
   - Temperature management: Day 25-30°C, Night 15-20°C
   - Artificial pollination necessary: Bees etc. insufficient in closed environment
   - Prevent etiolation: Proper thinning and training

3. **Root Vegetables (Radish, carrot, komatsuna, etc.)**
   - Soil compaction countermeasure: Regular loosening
   - Thinning: Ensuring appropriate spacing for growth
   - Harvest timing: Determining based on size and leaf condition

4. **Strawberries**
   - House cultivation: Harvest possible from Dec to May
   - Biennial cultivation: Technique for stable harvest every year
   - Pest and disease control: Watch for powdery mildew and gray mold

### Countermeasure Mindset (在りかた)

1. **Complete exclusion is difficult**: Wild boars have high learning ability, may reappear once accustomed

4. **Consult with administration**: Report damage to Munakata City Agricultural Committee, collect subsidy information

#### Note: Grafting vs. Cuttings

In this guide, "grafting" refers to the following:

**Grafting**:
- A technique for joining a scion branch to an existing rootstock
- Combining the root system of one tree with the fruit-bearing top of another
- Success rate: 60-85% (depending on graft type)
- Advantage: Fruits sooner, maintains quality of parent variety
- Use: Propagating named varieties, strengthening weak rootstocks

**Stem Cuttings**:
- A method of cutting a branch and rooting it in soil
- Does not require rootstock (becomes own-root tree)
- Success rate: Typically 30-50% (depends on variety)
- Advantage: Simpler setup, produces genetically identical own-root trees
- Use: Strawberries, some fruit trees, ornamental trees

### Propagation by Cuttings (Reference)

#### Peach Cuttings

**Best Season**: June-July (Summer)

**Procedure**:
1. Cut current-year healthy branches to 15-20cm length
2. Remove 2/3 of lower leaves
3. Apply rooting hormone (auxin)
4. Insert into pot with red soil or perlite
5. Manage at high temperature/humidity (25-30°C), mist spray for moisture
6. Roots appear in 2-4 weeks (success rate 30-50%)

#### Pear Cuttings

**Best Season**: December-February (Dormant Season)

**Procedure**:
1. Cut current-year branches to 20-25cm length
2. Leave 2-3 buds, remove lower leaves
3. Apply rooting hormone
4. Manage at low temperature (10-15°C)
5. Roots appear in 3-6 weeks (success rate 40-60%)
