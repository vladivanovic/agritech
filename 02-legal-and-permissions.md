## 1. Agricultural Land Act, Article 3 （農地法第3条）

### 1.1 What it is

Buying, being gifted, or leasing farmland **for cultivation** requires permission under Article 3(1) of the Agricultural Land Act. The transaction has no legal effect without it. Permission is **not** needed for the separate case of acquiring farmland by inheritance — that is a notification （第3条の3届出） instead, and it is not this project.

### 1.2 Who grants it

The **agricultural committee of the municipality where the land is located** — here, the **Munakata City Agricultural Committee （宗像市農業委員会）**, application addressed to its chairperson. Applications are filed at the **農業委員会事務局農地係**, Munakata City Hall, 宗像市東郷一丁目1番1号， **tel. 0940-36-0046**. (An earlier draft said "Prefectural Agricultural Committee" — no such body grants Article 3 permission. The prefecture's 農業会議 is an advisory organisation, a different thing.)

### 1.3 Who may apply — nationality and residence status

**農地法 sets no nationality or status-of-residence eligibility criterion.** Claims in the earlier draft that only Japanese nationals/permanent residents may acquire farmland, that work-visa holders are "NOT eligible", or that a "Special Agricultural Practitioner status" exists as a land route, were all wrong and have been deleted.

What does exist is *reporting*: since September 2023 the transferee's nationality must be stated on the application (for ownership transfers), and since April 2025 a foreign transferee also states their status of residence and its expiry — so the committee can see if the applicant is about to move away or their status will lapse with no prospect of renewal. A permanent resident is therefore straightforward in practice, but permanent residency is a practical convenience, not a legal eligibility test.

### 1.4 The permission criteria (as they now stand)

| Criterion | Meaning in practice |
|---|---|
| **全部効率利用** (use the whole parcel efficiently) | The plan must show the *entire* acquired land being farmed, with machinery, labour and technique appropriate to it |
| **農作業常時従事** (constant engagement) | The acquirer **or household members （世帯員等）** must be constantly engaged in the necessary farm work. Official working standard: **~150 days/year** (Munakata's own 営農計画書 states 年間150日以上）. Assessed for the household collectively, and fewer days can pass where the acreage genuinely requires less work — but see the honest discussion in [01-overview-and-strategy.md](01-overview-and-strategy.md) §4: for a side business this is the key approval consideration |
| **地域との調和** (harmony with the locality) | The new farming must not hinder surrounding agricultural use — e.g. blocking collective farming arrangements, or spray practices that damage neighbours |
| **農地所有適格法人要件** | Corporations only: extra conditions for a corporation to own farmland. Not applicable to an individual applicant |

**There is no minimum-area requirement.** The old 下限面積 rule (colloquially "50 ares") was **abolished on 1 April 2023**; municipal 別段面積 went with it. Earlier drafts listed "business scale 50+ ares" as a live criterion and even sized the orchard plan against it — deleted.

Speculative or investment-only acquisition remains impermissible: genuine cultivation intent is required.

### 1.5 Process and timing

1. **Land search & seller discussion** — via 全国農地ナビ (the national farmland information system), the むなかた地域農業活性化機構， JA, or agents.
2. **Pre-consultation with the Agricultural Committee secretariat** — strongly recommended; discuss the plan, the labour/engagement picture, and required documents before filing.
3. **Prepare the filing** — the city's forms: 許可申請書 (+別添）, 営農計画書， plus attachments (§1.6). The application is made **jointly by transferor and transferee**.
4. **Water-rights consent and district briefing** — obtain the 水利関係承諾書 from the local water-rights holders, and explain the application to the district's committee member / promotion member, who signs the 説明受書.
5. **File by the 25th** of a month → deliberation at the committee general meeting **around the 10th of the following month** → permission （許可書） issued. Realistic elapsed time ~2–6 weeks depending on where in the cycle you file. **No fee.**
6. **After permission** — complete the purchase and register the ownership transfer at the Legal Affairs Bureau; then farm the land as described in the filing.

A complete draft of the filing, mirroring the city's forms, is at [application/munakata-article3-submission.md](application/munakata-article3-submission.md).

### 1.7 Common mistakes

| Mistake | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Unrealistic income/expense figures in the plan | Use JA/official market prices (see finance doc); conservative numbers survive interview better |
| Claiming more farm days than a full-time job allows | State the real arithmetic (overview §4) |
| Tree counts that contradict the stated spacing | Make the arithmetic agree |
| Expired certificates | Registry/residence documents must be issued within 3 months of filing |
| Forgetting the water consent | It is a Munakata requirement, not an optional extra |

## 2. Water and irrigation — the correct picture

*(This section replaces a wholly invented one. The original described a "Munakata River", a city water department allocating river water, per-m³ tariffs, meters and monthly reports. None of that exists — FACTCHECK.md #35–38.)*

- Munakata's principal river is the **釣川 (Tsurikawa)**, a **Class-2 river managed by Fukuoka Prefecture** (the river administrator is the Governor), flowing through the city to the Genkai Sea.
- Taking river water is governed nationally by the **River Act （河川法） Article 23**: it requires the river administrator's permission. Long-standing agricultural use rests on **customary water rights （慣行水利権）**, treated as permitted.
- In the Tsurikawa basin, irrigation water is in practice held and managed **collectively**: the land improvement district （宗像地区土地改良区） holds River Act permissions for the system, and farmers take water as district members, paying **district levies （賦課金）** — not per-volume municipal fees.
- For a new orchard, the practical interface is exactly what the Article 3 checklist demands: the **水利関係承諾書**, consent of the local water-rights holders for the parcel. Ask the committee secretariat and the seller, before purchase, what water arrangements (district channel, pond, well) the specific parcel actually has.
- Unauthorised occupation of river water is a **criminal** offence under River Act Art. 102 (up to 1 year's imprisonment or a fine of up to ¥500,000).
- The city's drinking-water utility （宗像地区事務組合） is irrelevant to irrigation rights.

## 3. Business structure

For a side business of this scale, start as a **sole proprietor （個人事業）** and revisit only if the operation grows.

| | Sole proprietor | 合同会社 (GK) | 株式会社 (KK) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | File a 開業届 with the tax office — **free** | Registration licence tax min. ¥60,000 | Registration licence tax min. ¥150,000 (0.7% of capital), plus notary certification of articles (~¥15,000–50,000) and stamp duty; ~¥200,000–240,000 all-in |
| Liability | Unlimited personal liability | Limited to investment | Limited to investment |
| Tax on profit | Personal income tax (national 5–45% progressive, +10% resident tax) | Corporate taxes; a small corporation (capital ≤¥100M) pays a reduced **15% national rate on the first ¥8M of annual income** (special measure through FY2026), plus local corporate taxes — the headline "23.2%" is only the standard national rate | Same as GK |
| Consumption tax | Exempt while base-period taxable sales ≤ ¥10M | Same test | Same test |
| Fit here | **Yes — starting point** | Consider if liability/scale ever justifies it | Unlikely to be needed |

Corrections to the earlier draft: agriculture is **outside the scope of business tax （事業税）** altogether (the old "¥4.8M threshold" was meaningless), and filing as a sole proprietor costs nothing. Also note: farmland-owning corporations face the extra 農地所有適格法人 conditions — another reason the individual route is simpler.

Also relevant to a *side* business: employer rules. Many employers require notification/permission for side work （副業届） — check your own employer's policy before filing anything; that is an employment matter, not an agricultural-law one.

## 4. Selling the produce — food law basics

- **Selling fresh fruit** (JA consignment, 道の駅むなかた, farm stand, online) needs no food-business licence; JA/direct-sales channels have their own membership and consignment arrangements.
- **Processed foods** (jam, juice, dried fruit) are different: they fall under the Food Sanitation Act's **permit or notification categories** (the 2021 reform created specific categories for small-scale manufacturing such as jam-making), applied for at the prefectural **保健福祉（環境）事務所** with jurisdiction over the facility. The **permit fee is roughly ¥16,000** (manufacturing categories are in the ~¥14,000–26,000 band) with **no annual fee** and validity of 5+ years. Facility fit-out costs are construction costs, not licence costs — the earlier draft's "¥100,000–200,000 setup + ¥20,000/year licence" was wrong.
- **HACCP-based hygiene management** has been mandatory since June 2021 for food businesses, scaled to the operation's size.
- **Labelling** of processed food must show: product name, ingredients (with allergen labelling), net content, best-before date, storage conditions, and the manufacturer's name and address.
- There is no formal "small-scale home exemption" category — small producers still fall under a permit or notification category depending on the food. Confirm the correct category with the health centre *before* building anything.
