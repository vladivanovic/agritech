# Overview & Strategy — Fruit Trees as a Side Business in Munakata

*Corrected and reframed October 2026. This document replaces the original "agricultural management plan", which wrongly assumed a transition to full-time farming, leasing instead of buying, and revenue from trees in their planting year. See [FACTCHECK.md](FACTCHECK.md) for what was wrong and why it changed.*

---

## 1. The plan in one paragraph

Buy roughly **0.4 ha of farmland （畑） in Munakata City** and plant a small orchard — **mikan, peach and nashi pear** — run as a **side business alongside a full-time salaried job**. The salary funds the establishment years; the orchard is deliberately sized to what weekend-and-leave labour can genuinely manage. Fruit is sold locally through JAむなかた, 道の駅むなかた and direct sales once the trees come into bearing (light crops from Year 2–3, meaningful production from Year 4–5, maturity around Year 8).

This is **not** a career-change plan. Earlier drafts framed it as "transitioning to full-time farming"; that framing has been removed everywhere, because it was not true and because documents submitted to the city must describe the real situation.

## 2. Why buying, and what permission that needs

- The goal is a permanent orchard; trees are a 15–40-year asset, so the plan is to **buy** the land, not lease it. (Earlier drafts modelled a lease while also charging the "owner's" fixed asset tax — internally contradictory, and now removed.)
- A purchase of farmland for cultivation requires **permission under Article 3 of the Agricultural Land Act （農地法第3条第1項）**, granted by the **Munakata City Agricultural Committee （宗像市農業委員会）**. Without permission the sale is void. Full details: [02-legal-and-permissions.md](02-legal-and-permissions.md).
- The application is a **joint filing by seller and buyer**, decided at the committee's monthly general meeting. Applications received **by the 25th** of a month are deliberated at the meeting **around the 10th of the following month**. There is no application fee.
- A draft submission pack, mirroring the city's official forms item-for-item, is at [application/munakata-article3-submission.md](application/munakata-article3-submission.md).

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Year 0 | Permission, purchase, registration; soil preparation; boar fencing and irrigation installed; planting in the dormant season (Nov–Feb) |

## 4. The key approval consideration: 常時従事 (constant engagement)

This is the issue the whole application turns on, so it is stated plainly.

**The rule.** Article 3 permission requires that the acquirer **or their household members （世帯員等）** be *constantly engaged* （常時従事） in the farm work the land needs. MAFF and committee practice uses **~150 days/year** as the working standard, and Munakata City's own 営農計画書 form prints "年間150日以上常時従事が必要". Two nuances matter (both confirmed in FACTCHECK.md #6):

1. The day count is assessed for the **household collectively**, not the applicant alone.
2. The standard is about engagement in the work the holding **actually requires** — fewer days can be acceptable where the acreage genuinely needs less work, and the application's own form （別添， Art. 3(2)(iv) section) has a mechanism for this: where no individual reaches 150 days, you mark ○ for each person who engages *whenever the necessary farm work exists*.

**The honest arithmetic for a full-time worker:**

| Source of farm days | Realistic days/year |
|---|---|
| Weekends (Sat + Sun, ~52 weeks) | ~100 days available; not all will be farm days in practice |
| National holidays falling on weekdays | ~10–15 days |
| Paid leave deliberately taken at peak work (planting, thinning, harvest) | ~10 days |
| **Applicant total (realistic, sustainable)** | **~110–125 days** |
| Household member help at peaks (harvest, planting, packing) | ~40–60 days |
| **Household total** | **~150–180 days of engagement** |

**What this means, practically:**

- On paper the household total can reach the 150-day standard. But the committee will test plausibility at interview — a full-time employee claiming 200 personal farm days (as an earlier draft of the application did) would damage credibility. **State real numbers.**
- A 0.4 ha young orchard's genuine labour requirement in the establishment years is modest — that cuts both ways: it makes the plan honestly manageable, and it is the argument to make under nuance (2) above.
- Labour peaks do not respect weekends: peach thinning and bagging (Apr–Jun), harvest windows, and spray timing after rain are the pinch points. Mitigations are real but partial: variety choice that spreads harvest windows, taking leave at the two or three true peaks, and occasional hired casual labour at harvest (budgeted in the financial model from Year 3).
- Peach is the most time-critical crop in the mix. If the committee (or experience) suggests the labour plan is too thin, the fallback is to shrink or drop the peach block first — it is 0.1 ha and the plan survives without it.

**Bottom line:** pre-consultation with the Agricultural Committee secretariat (0940-36-0046) *before* filing is essential, presenting the side-business reality and the day-count arithmetic openly. This is flagged here — and in the submission draft — as the project's principal approval risk, ahead of money.

## 6. Indicative timeline

| When | Step |
|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Pre-consultation with the Agricultural Committee secretariat and the 機構； confirm the 常時従事 discussion and land requirements |
| Months 2–4 | Land search via 全国農地ナビ， the 機構 and JA; site visits; check water arrangements and district contacts for candidate plots |
| Months 4–5 | Agree terms with the seller; sound out the water-rights holders' consent （水利関係承諾書） and brief the district committee member （説明受書） |
| Month 5 | Sign a sale contract conditional on Article 3 permission; file the joint application **by the 25th** |
| Month 6 | Committee general meeting (~10th); permission; completion, payment and ownership registration |
| Months 6–9 | Soil preparation, fencing, irrigation; 開業届 filed (free) before sales begin |
| Nov–Feb | Planting |
| Years 1–3 | Establishment; join NOSAI/収入保険； build sales channels from first light crops |
| Years 4–5 | Ramp to roughly half production; review whether to expand, hold, or rebalance crops |

## 7. Principal risks

| Risk | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Permission refused or questioned on 常時従事 grounds** | The central risk; mitigated by honest numbers, small scale, and pre-consultation (§4) |
| Peach labour peaks vs a working week | Variety spread, leave planning, casual labour; peach block is the designated shrink-first element |
| Wild boar damage | Fencing before planting; see [operations/pest-and-wildlife.md](operations/pest-and-wildlife.md) |
| Typhoon / frost | Standard for Fukuoka fruit growing; insurance via 農業共済/収入保険； frost is mainly a peach-bloom issue |
| Prices lower than modelled | Model uses wholesale-level prices (FACTCHECK.md #49–50); direct sales are upside, not assumption |
| Subsidy assumptions | The base financial model assumes **no subsidy income at all** — see [03-finance-and-subsidies.md](03-finance-and-subsidies.md) for why |
