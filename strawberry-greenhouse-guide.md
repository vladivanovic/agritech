# Greenhouse Guide: Strawberries

## 1. Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆ (High - strawberry greenhouse staple in Japan)
- **Optimal temperature**: 16-24°C day, 8-13°C night
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 8b-9b (Munakata Zone 9b-10a - excellent match)
- **Growing season**: November-June (forcing culture in Japan; peak Nov-May)
- **Day length sensitivity**: Short days (12-14h) induce flowering; day-neutral varieties also available

## 3. Greenhouse Design for Strawberries

### 3.1 Required Specifications
| Specification | Requirement | Purpose |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **Height** | 2.0-2.5m | Plant training space; room for elevated beds |
| **Width** | 6-8m | 2-3 rows, aisles |
| **Length** | 20-30m | Flexible, depends on scale |
| **Ventilation** | 30-40 air exchanges/hour | Humidity control critical |
| **Temperature control** | 8-24°C range | Seasonal adjustment (heating in winter, ventilation in spring) |
| **Humidity** | 60-70% optimal | Below 80% prevents mildew/fruit rot |

Planting Configuration:
- **Elevated beds**: 30-40cm high, 50cm wide aisles
- **Plant density**: 25-30 plants per m² in gutters/towers
- **Gutter system**: 1-2 plants per 30cm gutter section
- **Aisle**: 1.0m between rows for access

**Elevated Bed Benefits**:
- Prevents fruit touch ground (reduces grey mold/rot)
- Ergonomic harvesting height (no bending required)
- Improved drainage and air circulation around plants
- Concreted floor recommended for easy cleaning and disinfection

### 3.3 Irrigation & Fertilization
- **Drip irrigation**: 1 emitter per plant, 1-2 L/hour flow rate
- **Fertigation schedule**:
  - Transplanting to flowering: Balanced N-P-K (e.g., 3-1-2)
  - Flowering to fruit set: Potassium-heavy (promotes fruit development)
  - Fruit enlargement: Consistent feeding with calcium
- **EC management**: 1.2-1.8 dS/m (monitor and adjust weekly)
- **pH management**: 5.8-6.2 optimal (monitor weekly)
- **Calcium supplementation**: Prevents fruit deformation and bitter pit; apply via irrigation or foliar spray

### 3.4 Pest & Disease Management
- **Major diseases**: 
  - Powdery mildew (white fuzzy growth on leaves)
  - Gray mold/botrytis (soft rot, brown lesions on fruit)
  - Root rot (Phytophthora) - wet conditions cause root decay
  - Anthracnose (dark sunken lesions on fruit)
- **Major pests**: 
  - Spider mites (tiny mites, webbing on leaves)
  - Aphids (cluster on new growth, suck sap)
  - Whitefly (small white flies, honeydew secretion)
  - Slugs/snails (feed on ripe fruit, especially in damp conditions)
- **Prevention**:
  - **60-70% humidity management** (CRITICAL - mildew risk above 80%)
  - Ventilation (horizontal airflow essential - HAF fans)
  - Remove infected leaves/fruit immediately (sanitation)
  - Yellow/blue sticky traps (monitor and reduce pest populations)
  - Beneficial insects (predatory mites for spider mites, parasitic wasps for whitefly)
  - Crop rotation (1+ year break between strawberry crops in same house)
  - Disease-free runners/plants (start with certified stock)
  - Remove old plant debris before new planting
- **Organic options**: 
  - Neem oil sprays (fungal control and insect suppression)
  - Potassium bicarbonate (powdery mildew control)
  - Bacillus subtilis (biocontrol agent for root rot and foliar diseases)

### 3.5 Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**:
  - Full color development (red for Amaou, Sachinoka; white for white varieties)
  - Easy separation from receptacle (gentle lift and twist)
  - Sugar content: 16-19°Brix optimal (measured with handheld refractometer)
  - Firm fruit but fully colored
- **Harvest frequency**: Every 2-3 days during peak season (Nov-May)
- **Yield**: 
  - 2-3kg per m² per season (elevated gutter system)
  - Approximately 1-1.5kg per plant annually
- **Storage**:
  - 1-2 weeks at 4-8°C, 90-95% humidity (refrigerate, but NOT below 4°C)
  - Do NOT wash until ready to sell/eat (moisture accelerates rot)
  - Highly perishable; sell within 2-3 days of harvest for best quality
  - Layer in shallow containers, do not stack deeply
- **Note for "Pick-Your-Own" (PYO) farms**:
  - Extremely popular in Munakata and surrounding areas
  - Visitors pick directly from elevated gutters
  - Price premium for experience; typically ¥1,000-2,000 per 100g container
  - Reservations required on weekends/holidays during peak season
  - Best season: Late Nov through May; peak Dec-Feb

### 3.6 Interior Design for Strawberry Greenhouses
- **Flooring**: **Concrete** recommended (easy to hose down, disinfect, prevents mud)
- **Walkways**: 1.0m wide concreted aisles between elevated beds
- **Benches/Gutters**: 30-40cm height, galvanized steel or UV-resistant plastic
- **Training system**: Gutters/towers at 30-40cm height; 1-2 plants per 30cm gutter
- **Climate control**: 
  - **Heating**: Propane heaters with thermostat ( winter months Nov-Feb)
  - **Cooling**: Side vents + roof vents + circulation fans (spring Mar-May)
  - **Humidity**: Dehumidification system critical (Jun-Optimal 60-70%)
- **Lighting**: Natural light supplemented with LED inter-lighting if growing through summer (Jun-Aug)
- **Irrigation setup**: Drip lines along each gutter row, individual emitters per plant
- **Work station**: One side of greenhouse dedicated to sorting/packing station with:
  - Refrigerated display case
  - Packaging materials (punnets, labels)
  - Hand-washing station
  - Small-scale refrigeration (5-10°C for short-term holding)

### 3.7 Year-Round Production Strategy (Japanese Forcing Culture)
| Season | Task | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Nov-Dec** | Planting (seedlings), begin heating, reduce day length | Start forcing culture |
| **Jan-Feb** | Peak harvest, maximum heating, pollination (bees or hand) | Coldest months, highest market prices |
| **Mar-Apr** | Gradual temperature reduction, pollination management | Transition season |
| **May-Jun** | Reduce water/heat, final harvest, remove plants | End of forcing cycle |
| **Jul-Aug** | Soil solarization, soil amendment, fallow period | Disease break |
| **Sep-Oct** | New seedling planting, prepare for next cycle | Restart forcing culture |
