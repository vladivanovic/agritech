# Field Guide: Potatoes

## 1. Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆ (High for Munakata, Fukuoka)
- **Optimal temperature**: 15-20°C tuber development; 20-25°C foliage growth
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 6a-9b (Munakata Zone 9b-10a - excellent)
- **Growing season**: Plant March-April (spring), harvest June-July; optional late summer planting for autumn crop
- **Rainfall**: 1,000-1,500mm adequately; good drainage essential (potatoes rot in waterlogged soil)

## 2. Planting

### 2.1 Seed Preparation (Tubers)
- **Seed tubers**: Small whole potatoes or cut pieces with 2-3 eyes each
- **Pre-sprouting (optional)**: 2-3 weeks in warm (15-20°C), bright location before planting to speed emergence
- **Cutting & curing**: If cutting large tubers, cut into 30-50g pieces with 2-3 eyes; dust with fungicide powder; cure 2-3 days at 15-20°C before planting

### 2.2 Field Preparation
- **Soil type**: Well-draining loam or sandy loam; pH 5.5-6.5 optimal
- **Bed preparation**: Raised beds 20-30cm high, 70-80cm wide, any length
- **Tilling**: 30-40cm deep, remove rocks/clods, incorporate organic matter
- **Fertilizer**: Base fertilizer - organic compost 10-15kg/m² + balanced fertilizer (N-P-K 8-8-8) 150g/m²; 
  - Side-dressing: Nitrogen 30g/m² at planting, side-dress 30 days after emergence

### 2.3 Planting Procedure
1. **Trench/furrow**: 8-10cm deep, 60-70cm between rows
2. **Plant spacing**: 25-30cm between tubers in row (whole small tubers) or 30-40cm between pieces
3. **Planting**: Tubers 25-30cm apart, eyes up; cover with 5-8cm soil
4. **Hilling**: As plants grow 20-30cm tall, mound soil around stem (cover half the plant); repeat 2-3 times during season
- **Watering**: Thorough watering after planting (30L/m²); consistent moisture during tuber formation

## 3. Maintenance

### 3.1 Hilling (Critical for Potato Production)
- **First hilling**: When plants are 20-30cm tall; mound soil around base, covering lower leaves
- **Second hilling**: 2-3 weeks later; mound higher, leaving only top leaves exposed
- **Purpose**: 
  - Protects developing tubers from sunlight (prevents greening/toxicity)
  - Encourages more tuber formation along buried stem
  - Improves drainage, reduces disease risk

### 3.2 Fertilization Schedule
- **Base** (bed prep): Organic compost 10-15kg/m² + balanced fertilizer 150g/m² (N-P-K 8-8-8)
- **Side-dress 1** (planting): Nitrogen 30g/m² to promote foliage growth
- **Side-dress 2** ( tuber initiation, ~6 weeks): Potassium 50g/m² (supports tuber development); reduce nitrogen
- **Side-dress 3** (if needed, late season): Minor amounts only; excessive nitrogen reduces tuber quality

### 3.3 Weed Control
- **Hand weeding**: Required until canopy closes (6-8 weeks)
- **Cultivation**: Light hoeing between rows (hilling also controls weeds)
- **Mulch**: Straw or black plastic mulch suppresses weeds and conserves moisture

### 3.3 Irrigation Schedule
- **Total water requirement**: 450-600mm throughout growing season
- **Weekly requirement**: 15-25mm water (equivalent to 150-250L/m²) during active growth and tuber formation
- **Critical periods**: 
  - Planting to emergence: Consistent moisture (not waterlogged)
  - Tuber initiation to maturation: Moderate moisture; avoid drought stress during tuber bulking
  - 2 weeks before harvest: Reduce water to harden skins, prevent rot
- **Method**: Drip irrigation or furrow irrigation; avoid overhead irrigation if possible (foliage diseases)

### 3.4 Moisture Stress Signs
- **Too much water**: Yellowing leaves, soft tubos, fungal rot, misshapen tubers
- **Too little water**: Small tubers, reduced yield, premature foliage die-back, lower quality

## 4. Pest & Disease Management

### 4.1 Common Pests
| Pest | Damage | Control |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Colorado potato beetle**: Yellow-orange with black stripes, defoliates plants | Hand-pick, neem oil, Spinosad, row covers (prevent arrival) |
| **Aphids**: Cluster on leaves, suck sap; transmit viruses | Insecticidal soap, neem oil, beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings) |
| **Wireworms**: Eat tubers, make holes; click beetle larvae | Crop rotation, soil cultivation, trap crops (potato tubers bait) |
| **Slugs/snails**: Eat tubers, leave slime trails | Remove hiding places, bait (iron phosphate), traps (beer bottles) |

### 4.2 Common Diseases
| Disease | Symptoms | Control |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Early blight**: Target-shaped dark spots on leaves, spreads quickly in warm, humid | Copper fungicides, crop rotation (3+ years), remove infected debris |
| **Late blight**: Dark water-soaked spots, rapid leaf death, tuber rot (late season) | Copper/fungicide sprays, remove cull piles, late harvest varieties resistant | 
| **Common scab**: Raised, rough patches on tuber skin | Soil pH below 5.5 avoided; lime to raise pH; resistant varieties |
| **Powdery scab**: Raised, corky lesions on tubers | Similar to common scab; improve drainage, resistant varieties |
| **Soft rot**: Wet, mushy rot in storage; enters through wounds | Cure properly (dry 2-3 weeks, 25-30°C, good airflow), remove diseased, careful handling |

### 4.2 Protection Methods
- **Crop rotation**: 3+ years before replanting potatoes in same field
- **Certified seed**: Use disease-free certified seed tubers (not saved from previous crop)
- **Sanitation**: Remove cull piles, volunteer potatoes, infected plant material
- **Crop sequence**: Good followers: leafy greens, legumes, corn, cereals; Avoid: other nightshades (tomato, pepper, eggplant), strawberries
- **Resistant varieties**: Some varieties have tolerance to early/late blight

## 5. Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: 
  - Foliage yellows and dies back (90-120 days after planting for maincrop)
  - Skin set (rub skin gently; doesn't slip off)
  - Tuber size desired (wait for size or foliage die-back)
- **Harvest timing**: 
  - Early harvest ("new potatoes"): 70-90 days, before full maturity
  - Maincrop: 90-120 days after planting, after foliage die-back
- **Harvest method**: 
  - Dig with fork or spade, 30cm from plant center to avoid cutting tubers
  - Lift carefully, shake off excess soil
- **Cure**: 
  - 10-14 days at 15-20°C, high humidity (80-90%), dark place
  - Hardens skin, improves storage life
- **Storage**: 
  - 3-6 months at 7-10°C, 90-95% humidity (dark place)
  - Do NOT refrigerate (cold-induced sweetening, texture changes)
  - Keep away from onions (ethylene/sprouting)
  - Check regularly, remove any rotting tubers
- **Yield**: 
  - 10-20kg per m² (maincrop, depending on variety and conditions)
  - 1-2kg per m² for early harvest ("new potatoes")

## 5. Field Layout & Rotation
- **Rotation**: 3+ years before replanting potatoes in same field
- **Good followers**: Leafy greens, legumes, corn, cereals, onions
- **Avoid following**: Other nightshades (tomato, pepper, eggplant), strawberries, other potatoes (shared disease risk)
- **Intercropping**: Can plant between rows early season (lettuce, spinach, radish); remove as canopy closes
