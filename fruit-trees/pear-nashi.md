# Fruit Tree Guide — Pear / Nashi （梨）

*One of three fruit-tree guides in this project; all three use the same structure. Planning figures match [03-finance-and-subsidies.md](../03-finance-and-subsidies.md).*

## Overview

- **Crop:** 梨 / nashi (*Pyrus pyrifolia*), deciduous pome fruit.
- **Fit for Munakata:** ★★★★☆ — well suited; long-lived trees (30–50 years) make nashi a good "plant once" crop for a side business.
- **In this plan:** 0.1 ha (1,000 m²), **40 trees at 5 m × 5 m spacing**, with varieties interplanted for pollination.
- **Climate needs:** Growing-season optimum 20–30 °C; handles Kyushu summer heat well; good drainage still required.

## Varieties

| Variety | Harvest (Fukuoka) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 幸水 (Kōsui) | Aug | Sweet, juicy; the early-season standard |
| 豊水 (Hōsui) | **Mid-Aug – mid-Sep** | Large, brown skin, 13–15 Brix. *(An earlier draft of this guide said "October" — wrong, and contradicted by the same guide's calendar; corrected per FACTCHECK.md #56)* |
| 二十世紀 (Nijisseiki) | Sep | Crisp, yellow-green; the classic variety |
| 新高 (Niitaka) | Oct | Very large fruit; late season, good storage |

**Pollination is not optional:** most nashi varieties are self-unfruitful. Plant at least two compatible varieties together (the mix above does this) and encourage bees at bloom; hand/assisted pollination is common practice in Japanese nashi growing.

## Site & soil

- Full sun; well-drained loam, pH ~6.0–6.5.
- At planting: ~50% native soil + 30% compost + 20% coarse sand where drainage needs help.
- Nashi is **drought-sensitive during fruit enlargement** (Jun–Aug) — site it where irrigation reaches easily.
- Allow for a support structure: nashi in Japan is commonly grown on a **flat pergola trellis （棚仕立て）**, which suits even fruit sizing and easier picking; a free-standing central-leader form is the lower-cost alternative for a small block.

## Planting

- **When:** Dormant season, Dec–Feb.
- **Hole:** ~60 cm × 60 cm.
- Stake young trees (1.5 m stake, first ~3 years) — nashi grows vigorously upright.
- Water in well (~40 L); mulch.

## Growing & care

- **Fertilisation (guide):** base organic matter at planting; pre-bloom (Feb); light post-flower feed (May — excess nitrogen spoils colour); post-harvest recovery feed (Oct–Nov).
- **Thinning:** Apr–May, finish at roughly **1 fruit per 25–30 cm** of bearing wood / per fruit cluster reduced to one — essential for size.
- **Bagging:** Common for premium finish and pest protection; labour-heavy, apply selectively on a side-business schedule.
- **Water:** The most irrigation-dependent crop in this plan during enlargement; do not let the block dry out Jun–Aug.
- Consistent thinning also mitigates **alternate (biennial) bearing**.

## Pruning & training

- If on a pergola （棚）: train 2–4 main branches out along the wires and manage lateral fruiting wood on the flat canopy.
- If free-standing: central-leader/modified-leader form with well-spaced scaffolds.
- Winter prune (Dec–Feb): remove water sprouts, crossing wood, and renew fruiting laterals; nashi fruits on short spurs and one-year laterals depending on variety — keep a balance of young wood.

## Pest & disease management

| Threat | Notes / control |
|---|---|
| 黒星病 (pear scab) | The key disease; fallen-leaf sanitation + registered fungicides at label timings |
| 赤星病 (pear rust) | Needs juniper hosts nearby — check surroundings; fungicide cover in spring |
| ナシキイロアザミウマ / シンクイムシ類 | Monitoring; bagging helps; registered insecticides if needed |
| カイガラムシ (scale), アブラムシ (aphids) | Dormant oil; monitor |
| 火傷病 (fire blight) | Rare in Japan but devastating; remove and burn infected wood well below the lesion; sterilise tools |

No licence is needed to spray your own crop — label compliance under the 農薬取締法 is the obligation. Keep spray records.

| Jul | Enlargement continues; scab watch in wet weather |

| Sep | **Harvest: 豊水 to mid-Sep; 二十世紀** |
| Oct | **Harvest: 新高**; post-harvest feed begins |

| Dec | **Planting season**; winter pruning begins |

## Harvest

- Pick by variety indicators: 幸水 when fully coloured and sweet (it does not store long); 豊水 mid-Aug–mid-Sep; 新高 latest and stores best.
- Nashi is picked ripe-ready (unlike European pears) — judge by colour, ease of lift-and-twist, and taste.
- Storage: weeks to a few months at 0–5 °C depending on variety (late varieties best); keep away from strong ethylene sources.

## Risks & notes

- **Scab and rust** are weather-driven; a wet spring means a strict spray calendar — a real scheduling pressure for a weekend grower.
- **Pollination failure** (cold/wet bloom weather, too few bees) can cut a year's set; the variety mix and assisted pollination are the mitigations.
- Long tree life cuts both ways: variety mistakes are with you for decades — buy nursery stock carefully.
