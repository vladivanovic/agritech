# Fruit Tree Guide — Peach （桃）

*One of three fruit-tree guides in this project; all three use the same structure. Planning figures match [03-finance-and-subsidies.md](../03-finance-and-subsidies.md).*

## Overview

- **Crop:** 桃 (*Prunus persica*), deciduous stone fruit.
- **Fit for Munakata:** ★★★☆☆ — workable, but the most demanding crop in this plan: late-winter frost can destroy blossom, and thinning/bagging create sharp labour peaks that clash with a working week. This is the block to shrink first if labour proves too thin (see the overview doc).
- **In this plan:** 0.1 ha (1,000 m²), **40 trees at 5 m × 5 m spacing**.
- **Climate needs:** Warm summers suit Kyushu; winter chill requirement is modest for the standard Japanese varieties; **drainage is critical** — peaches hate wet roots.

## Varieties

*The original draft listed "Red Jade / Rising Sun / Summer Glow" — not recognised Japanese commercial cultivars (FACTCHECK.md #57). Use real ones:*

| Variety | Harvest (Fukuoka) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 白鳳 (Hakuhō) | Early–mid Jul | The standard early-mid variety; soft, juicy, white flesh |
| あかつき (Akatsuki) | Mid–late Jul | Firm, sweet; good keeping quality for a peach |
| 川中島白桃 (Kawanakajima Hakutō) | Early–mid Aug | Large, late-season; spreads the harvest window |

Staggering early/mid/late varieties is deliberate: it spreads both frost risk and the harvest peak.

## Site & soil

- The best-drained spot on the plot — a slight mound or slope; never a hollow where water or cold air sits.
- Deep, loose soil; at planting ~50% native soil + 30% compost + 20% coarse sand where drainage needs help.
- Frost: blossom (late Feb–Mar) is the vulnerable stage; avoid frost pockets.

## Planting

- **When:** Dormant season, Dec–Feb, before bud swell.
- **Hole:** ~60 cm diameter × 50 cm deep; plant on a slight mound for drainage.
- Water in well (~40 L); mulch; support if exposed.

## Growing & care

- **Fertilisation (guide):** base organic matter at planting; pre-bloom feed (Feb); light nitrogen after thinning (May — excess nitrogen damages fruit quality); post-harvest recovery feed (Aug).
- **Thinning — the critical job:** two passes (Apr–May), finishing at roughly **1 fruit per 20–30 cm of bearing wood**. Skipping thinning means small, poor fruit and broken branches.
- **Bagging:** Individual fruit bags (Jun) protect against モモシンクイ (peach fruit moth), splitting and disease — effective but labour-heavy; on a side-business schedule, bag the best-placed fruit and accept a lower pack-out on the rest.
- **Water:** Consistent moisture in Year 1; fruit enlargement (Jun–Jul) needs water in dry spells; stop excess water near harvest (sugar dilution, splitting).

## Pruning & training

- **Open-centre form:** 3–4 scaffold branches, no central leader — the standard for peach in Japan.
- Peaches fruit on **one-year-old wood**: prune annually in winter (Dec–Feb) to renew fruiting wood — harder and more often than mikan or pear.
- Post-harvest tidy (Aug): remove spent and crowded shoots.

## Pest & disease management

| Threat | Notes / control |
|---|---|
| 縮葉病 (leaf curl) | Dormant Bordeaux/lime-sulfur spray **before bud swell** is the standard control |
| 灰星病・褐腐病 (brown rot) | Remove mummified fruit; sanitation; registered fungicides at label timings |
| せん孔細菌病 (bacterial spot) | Copper at dormant timings; avoid working wet trees |
| モモシンクイ (peach fruit moth) | Bagging; pheromone disruption where used; prompt removal of infested fruit |
| カイガラムシ (scale), アブラムシ (aphids) | Dormant oil; monitor new growth |

No licence is needed to spray your own crop — label compliance under the 農薬取締法 is the obligation. Keep spray records.

## Maintenance calendar

| Month | Tasks |
|---|---|
| Jan | Winter pruning; dormant sprays |
| Feb | Finish pruning; leaf-curl spray before bud swell; pre-bloom fertiliser |
| Mar | **Bloom — frost protection watch** (fans/heaters/sprinklers if equipped); pollination weather critical |
| Apr | Fruit set; first thinning pass |
| May | Second thinning pass; post-thinning feed; pest monitoring |
| Jun | Bagging; fruit enlargement; irrigation in dry spells |
| Jul | **Harvest (early/mid varieties)**; pick firm-ripe, sell within days |
| Aug | **Harvest (late variety)**; post-harvest pruning and feed |
| Sep | Tree recovery; moderate water/fertiliser management |
| Oct | Soil work; compost; plan winter jobs |
| Nov | Leaf fall; sanitation — remove mummies and fallen leaves |
| Dec | **Planting season**; winter pruning begins |

## Harvest

- Pick **firm-ripe**: background colour changed, flesh just yielding. Peaches ripen off the tree but flavour is set at picking.
- Shelf life is short: days, not weeks. Sell within 3–7 days of harvest; cold-store at 0–5 °C only briefly.
- Harvest runs late Jun–mid Aug across the three varieties — weekend-manageable only because the block is small.

## Risks & notes

- **Blossom frost (Mar)** can take a whole year's crop in one night; it is the crop's defining risk in Munakata.
- **Labour peaks** (thinning Apr–May, bagging Jun, harvest Jul–Aug) are the tightest in the plan and the main reason this block is small.
- Short tree life (15–20 years) means replanting decisions come sooner than for mikan or pear.
