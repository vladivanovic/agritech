# Per-Fruit Cultivation Guides: Climate, Care, Protection & Growth Patterns

## 1. Mandarin/Mikan (Citrus unshiu)

### 1.1 Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★★ (Best for Munakata, Fukuoka)
- **Temperature Range**: 15-28°C optimal; tolerates 0-35°C but growth slows outside 10-30°C
- **Rainfall**: 1,500-2,000mm annually; Munakata receives ~1,800mm - suitable
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 9a-9b; Munakata is Zone 9b-10a
- **Chill Hours**: Requires 200-400 hours below 7°C for flower bud differentiation; Munakata has mild winters (sufficient)

### 1.2 Recommended Varieties for Munakata
| Variety | Characteristics | Harvest Season | Notes |
|---------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| **Mikan (Standard)** | Sweet, easy-peel, standard size | Nov-Jan | Most common; prefecture specialty |
| **Harumikan** | Early ripening, high quality | Mar-Apr | Premium; good for early market |
| **Kiyomi** | Large fruit, less acidity, aromatic | Dec-Jan | Cross between mikan & orange |
| **Asami** | High sugar, very juicy | Feb-Mar | Premium variety; limited availability |

### 1.3 Planting & Care

**Planting Timeline:**
- **Optimal**: Dormant season Nov-Feb
- **Avoid**: Off-season (flowering/fruiting periods)
- **Seedling age**: 1-year or 2-year-old branches recommended

**Planting Procedure:**
1. **Hole preparation**: 50cm×50cm×50cm hole
2. **Soil mix**: 60% native soil + 30% compost + 10% river sand for drainage
3. **Planting depth**: Root ball top level with ground surface
4. **Watering**: Thorough watering after planting; 30L per seedling
5. **Mulching**: straw or wood chips 5-8cm depth around base

**Annual Care Calendar:**
| Month | Task |
|-------|------|
| **Nov-Feb** | Planting, base fertilizer (compost), mulching |
| **Mar** | Spring top-dressing (nitrogen促进 shoot growth) |
| **Apr-May** | Flower thinning (remove weak flowers), pest monitoring |
| **Jun** | Summer care, ensure water supply, bag fruit if desired |
| **Jul-Aug** | Fruit enlargement, bagging (optional for mikan), pest control |
| **Sep-Oct** | Early varieties harvest begin, leaf removal for sun exposure |
| **Nov** | Harvest late varieties, post-harvest fertilizer application |

**Fertilization Schedule:**
- **Base fertilizer** (planting): Organic compost 10-20kg/m²
- **Spring** (Mar): Nitrogen-rich fertilizer 50-100g N per tree
- **Summer** (Jun-Jul): Balanced N-P-K 100-150g per tree
- **Post-harvest** (Nov): Potassium-rich fertilizer 100-150g per tree

### 1.4 Growth Patterns
- **Year 1**: Root establishment; minimal harvesting (remove flowers/fruit)
- **Year 2-3**: First light harvest; tree structure development
- **Year 4-5**: Full production; 50-80kg per tree annually
- **Mature (Year 6+)**: 80-150kg per tree; peak production

### 1.5 Protection & Pest Management

**Common Pests:**
- **Citrus leafminer**: Larvae mine leaves; control with neem oil or spinosad
- **Asian citrus psyllid**: Vector of Huangbingbing disease; monitor with yellow sticky traps
- **Aphids**: New shoot infestation; control with insecticidal soap
- **Spider mites**: Dry condition proliferation; maintain humidity, water spraying

**Common Diseases:**
- **Citrus canker**: Bacterial; remove infected branches, copper fungicide spray
- **Brown rot**: Fungal; improve air circulation, remove mummified fruit
- **Root rot (Phytophthora)**: Overwatering; ensure drainage, avoid wet conditions

**Protection Methods:**
- **Bagging**: Individual fruit bags (paper or nylon) prevent pest damage and sunburn
- **Shoji nets**: 3mm mesh over orchard excludes birds and larger pests
- **Band trapping**: Sticky bands around trunk prevent ant climbing
- **Habitat management**: Maintain ground cover to beneficial insects

### 1.6 Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: Color (deep orange-yellow), ease of separation from tree
- **Season**: Main crop Nov-Jan; early varieties Oct; late varieties Feb
- **Storage**: 1-2 months at 7-10°C, 90-95% humidity
- **Post-harvest care**: Remove stems, gentle handling, quick cooling

## 2. Peach (Prunus persica)

### 2.1 Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★☆☆ (Moderate - frost risk is primary concern)
- **Temperature Range**: 
  - Growing season: 25-35°C optimal
  - Winter chilling: 200-400 hours below 7°C required (Munakata: ~300 hours - marginal)
  - Flowering risk: Late Feb-Mar; frost can destroy blossoms
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 6a-8a; Munakata Zone 9b makes late frost the main risk
- **Rainfall**: 1,500-2,000mm; good drainage essential (peaches hate wet roots)

### 2.2 Recommended Varieties for Munakata
| Variety | Characteristics | Harvest Season | Notes |
|---------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| **Red Jade** | Flesh soft, high sugar, red skin | July | Good for fresh market |
| **Rising Sun** | High sweetness, June harvest | June | Early variety; avoids some frost risk |
| **Summer Glow** | Extended harvest, July-August | Jul-Aug | Longest harvest period |

### 2.3 Planting & Care

**Planting Timeline:**
- **Optimal**: Dormant season Dec-Feb (before bud swell)
- **Avoid**: Spring planting (insufficient root establishment)
- **Tree form**: Open center (typical for peaches); 3-4 main scaffold branches

**Planting Procedure:**
1. **Hole**: 60cm diameter × 50cm deep
2. **Soil**: 50% native + 30% compost + 20% sharp sand (critical drainage)
3. **Mound planting**: Slight mound to improve drainage (peaches grafted on own roots)
4. **Watering**: 40L at planting; consistent moisture first year

**Annual Care Calendar:**
| Month | Task |
|-------|------|
| **Dec-Feb** | Planting (dormant), winter pruning, dormant oil spray |
| **Mar** | Bloom period; frost protection essential (fans, heaters, sprinklers) |
| **Apr** | Fruit set thinning; remove damaged/diseased fruit |
| **May** | Continue thinning; 1 fruit per 20-30cm branch length |
| **Jun** | Fruit enlargement; bagging optional (protects from split, pests) |
| **Jul** | Harvest (variety-dependent: June=early, July=main, Aug=late) |
| **Aug** | Post-harvest pruning; remove spent branches |
| **Sep-Nov** | Tree recovery; moderate fertilization, water management |

**Fertilization Schedule:**
- **Base** (planting): Organic compost 10-15kg/m² hole
- **Pre-bloom** (Feb): Phosphorus & potassium 100g per tree
- **Post-thinning** (May): Nitrogen 50g per tree (moderate - too much reduces fruit quality)
- **Post-harvest** (Aug): Potassium 100g per tree (tree recovery)

### 2.4 Growth Patterns
- **Year 1**: Root establishment; NO fruit harvest (remove all flowers)
- **Year 2**: Light harvest possible; 5-10kg per tree if thinned
- **Year 3-4**: Moderate harvest; 15-30kg per tree
- **Year 5+**: Full production; 30-50kg per tree annually
- **Mature tree lifespan**: 15-20 years with proper care

### 2.5 Protection & Pest Management

**Common Pests:**
- **Peach twig borer**: Larms bore into shoots and fruit; pheromone traps, Bacillus thuringiensis sprays
- **San Jose scale**: Armored scale on branches; horticultural oil (dormant + growing season)
- **Aphids**: New growth; insecticidal soap or neem oil
- **Japanese beetle**: Adults eat leaves; hand-pick, neem oil

**Common Diseases:**
- **Peach leaf curl**: Fungal; Bordeaux mixture spray (dormant, before bud swell)
- **Brown rot**: Fungal; fruit mummies removal, copper fungicide, improve air flow
- **Bacterial spot**: Bacterial; copper sprains, avoid working when wet
- **Root rot**: Phytophthora; ensure drainage, avoid overwatering

**Protection Methods:**
- **Frost protection**: 
  - Orchard fans air circulation
  - Wind machines (horizontal air movement)
  - Orchard heaters (propane or electric)
- **Rain cover**: Post-harvest protection from rain-induced split
- **Tree wrapping**: Whitewash trunk to prevent sunscald
- **Bagging**: Individual fruit bags prevent split, insect damage

### 2.6 Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: Color development, gentle twist separation, background color change
- **Season**: Variety-dependent (June: Rising Sun, July: Red Jade/Summer Glow)
- **Eating ripeness**: Softens at room temp; harvest when firm-ripe
- **Storage**: 1-2 weeks at 0-5°C; ripen at room temp as needed
- **Note**: Peaches have short shelf life; sell within 3-7 days of harvest

## 3. Pear (Japanese Pear / Nashi - Pyrus pyrifolia)

### 3.1 Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆ (High - well-suited to Munakata conditions)
- **Temperature Range**:
  - Growing season: 20-30°C optimal
  - Winter chilling: 200-300 hours below 7°C (Munakata: ~300 hours - sufficient)
  - Heat tolerance: Good; handles 35°C summer days well
- **Rainfall**: 1,500-2,000mm; good, but drainage essential
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 7b-9b; Munakata Zone 9b-10a - excellent match

### 3.2 Recommended Varieties for Munakata
| Variety | Characteristics | Harvest Season | Notes |
|---------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| **Hosui** | Explosive sweetness, large, brown skin | October | Most popular; high sugar (13-15 Brix) |
| **Nashi (20th Century)** | Hard flesh, crisp, yellow-green skin | Aug-Sep | Classic; good storage variety |
| **Shinseki** | Strong acidity, aromatic, Sep-Oct | Sep-Oct | Good balance sweet-acid; mid-season |

### 3.3 Planting & Care

**Planting Timeline:**
- **Optimal**: Dormant season Dec-Feb
- **Tree form**: Central leader; requires staking first 3-5 years
- **Pollination**: Most varieties self-unfruitful; need 2+ compatible varieties for best crop

**Planting Procedure:**
1. **Hole**: 60cm diameter × 60cm deep (deeper than peach/peach)
2. **Soil**: 50% native + 30% compost + 20% river sand; pH 6.0-6.5 optimal
3. **Staking**: 1.5m stake for first 3 years; pears grow vigorous upright
4. **Watering**: 40L at planting; consistent moisture first 2-3 years

**Annual Care Calendar:**
| Month | Task |
|-------|------|
| **Dec-Feb** | Planting (dormant), structural pruning, dormant spray |
| **Mar** | Bloom period; monitor for frost (less risk than peach) |
| **Apr** | Fruit set thinning; 1 fruit per 30-40cm branch |
| **May** | Canopy management; scaffold branch training |
| **Jun** | Fruit enlargement; ensure water (drought sensitive) |
| **Jul** | Continue sizing; pest/disease monitoring |
| **Aug-Sep** | **Hosui harvest** (Oct); **Nashi harvest** (Aug-Sep); **Shinseki** (Sep-Oct) |
| **Oct** | Main harvest period complete; post-harvest care |
| **Nov** | Post-harvest fertilizer, soil amendment |

**Fertilization Schedule:**
- **Base** (planting): Organic compost 15-20kg/m² hole
- **Pre-bloom** (Feb): Nitrogen 50g, Phosphorus 80g, Potassium 80g per tree
- **Post-flower** (May): Nitrogen 50g (moderate; too much reduces color)
- **Post-harvest** (Oct-Nov): Potassium 100g, Phosphorus 80g (tree recovery)

### 3.4 Growth Patterns
- **Year 1**: Root establishment; NO fruit harvest
- **Year 2**: Light harvest; 10-20kg per tree if thinned
- **Year 3-4**: Moderate harvest; 20-40kg per tree
- **Year 5+**: Full production; 50-100kg per tree annually
- **Mature tree lifespan**: 30-50 years; pears are long-lived
- **Alternate bearing**: Some varieties produce heavy crop one year, light next; consistent thinning mitigates

### 3.5 Protection & Pest Management

**Common Pests:**
- **Pear psylla**: Sucks sap, produces honeydew; yellow sticky traps, neem oil sprays
- **San Jose scale**: Same as peach; horticultural oil treatments
- **Aphids**: New growth; same controls
- **Codling moth**: Larvae in fruit; pheromone traps, spinosad sprays

**Common Diseases:**
- **Fire blight**: Bacterial; immediately remove infected tissue, copper sprays, antibiotic (if registered)
- **Pear scab**: Fungal; fallen leaf removal, fungicide sprays (captan, chlorothalonil)
- **Brown spot**: Fungal; similar to scab; improved sanitation
- **Powdery mildew**: Fungal; improve air circulation, fungicide if severe
- **Root rot**: Phytophthora; drainage essential, avoid waterlogging

**Protection Methods:**
- **Leaf removal**: Open canopy improves spray penetration & drying
- **Ground cover**: Maintain vegetation to reduce erosion, support beneficial insects
- **Sanitation**: Remove all fallen leaves/fruit mummies before winter
- **Trunk wrapping**: Whitewash防止 sunscald and rodent damage

### 3.6 Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: 
  - Color change (Hosui: brown-yellow; Nashi: yellow-green)
  - Ease of separation (twist-and-lift)
  - Sugar content: 12-15 Brix for Hosui
- **Season**: 
  - Nashi (20th Century): Aug-Sep
  - Hosui: October
  - Shinseki: Sep-Oct
- **Storage**:
  - 3-6 months at 3-7°C, 90-95% humidity
  - Sugar increases during storage (better flavor 1-2 months in storage)
  - Separate by variety; do not store with ethylene-producing fruits
- **Quality**: Best eaten 1-3 months after harvest; flavor improves with storage
