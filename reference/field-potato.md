# Reference — Field Guide: Potatoes

*Secondary/reference material — field vegetables are not part of the core orchard plan. Tidied October 2026; variety names and a water-unit error corrected per FACTCHECK.md #58–59.*

## 1. Climate suitability

- **Rating:** ★★★★☆ for Munakata
- **Optimal temperature:** 15–20 °C for tuber development; 20–25 °C for foliage
- **Season:** Spring crop — plant Feb–Mar in Kyushu (the original draft's "Mar–Apr" is late for Fukuoka), harvest May–Jun; an autumn crop is also possible
- **Rainfall:** 1,000–1,500 mm adequate; drainage essential (tubers rot in waterlogged soil)

## 3. Planting

### 3.1 Seed preparation

- Use **certified seed tubers**. Cut large tubers into 30–50 g pieces with 2–3 eyes each; cure cut pieces 2–3 days at 15–20 °C before planting.
- Optional pre-sprouting: 2–3 weeks in a warm (15–20 °C), bright place.

### 3.2 Field preparation

- **Soil:** well-drained loam or sandy loam, pH 5.5–6.5.
- Till 30–40 cm deep; incorporate organic matter (compost 10–15 kg/m²) plus balanced fertiliser (e.g. 8-8-8 ~150 g/m²) per soil test.
- Beds/ridges 70–80 cm apart.

### 3.3 Procedure

1. Furrows 8–10 cm deep, rows 60–70 cm apart.
2. Tubers 25–30 cm apart in-row, eyes up; cover with 5–8 cm of soil.
3. Water in (~30 L/m²); keep moisture consistent through emergence and tuber formation.

## 4. Maintenance

### 4.1 Hilling (critical)

- **First hilling** when plants are 20–30 cm tall; **second** 2–3 weeks later.
- Purpose: protects tubers from sunlight (greening/solanin), encourages tuber set along the buried stem, improves drainage.

### 4.2 Fertilisation

- **Base:** as §3.2.
- **Side-dress 1** (at planting/emergence): nitrogen ~30 g/m².
- **Side-dress 2** (tuber initiation, ~6 weeks): potassium ~50 g/m²; ease off nitrogen (excess lowers tuber quality).

### 4.3 Weed control

Hand-weed until canopy closure (6–8 weeks); hilling itself suppresses weeds; straw or black mulch helps.

### 4.4 Water

- **Season total:** ~450–600 mm.
- **Weekly during active growth and bulking:** **15–25 mm — that is 15–25 L/m²** (1 mm over 1 m² = 1 litre). *(The original draft said "150–250 L/m²" — the same ×10 unit error as the onion guide.)*
- Reduce water in the last ~2 weeks to harden skins.
- Drip or furrow irrigation preferred; avoid overhead watering (foliage disease).

## 5. Pest and disease management

| Threat | Damage | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Colorado potato beetle | Defoliation | Hand-pick, row covers, spinosad/neem |
| Aphids | Sap-sucking; virus vector | Soap sprays, beneficial insects |
| Wireworms | Holes in tubers | Rotation, cultivation, bait traps |
| Slugs/snails | Tuber feeding | Remove shelter, iron-phosphate bait, traps |
| Early blight | Target-spot leaf lesions | Copper cover, 3+ year rotation, debris removal |
| Late blight | Rapid leaf death, tuber rot | Fungicide cover, resistant varieties, remove cull piles |
| Common scab | Rough raised patches on skin | Keep soil pH from rising (scab worsens in alkaline soil); resistant varieties |
| Soft rot | Mushy storage rot | Careful handling, proper curing, cull ruthlessly |

Rotation: 3+ years before potatoes (or other nightshades — tomato, pepper, aubergine) return to the same ground. Use certified seed every year; do not replant your own harvest as seed.

## 6. Harvest and post-harvest

- **Indicators:** foliage yellows and dies back; skin is set (doesn't slip when rubbed).
- **Timing:** new potatoes from ~70–90 days; maincrop 90–120 days, after die-back.
- **Method:** dig with a fork ~30 cm from the plant centre; lift carefully, shake off soil.
- **Cure:** 10–14 days at 15–20 °C, high humidity (80–90%), dark.
- **Storage:** 3–6 months at 7–10 °C, 90–95% humidity, dark. Do not refrigerate (cold sweetening). Keep away from onions.
- **Yield:** ~3–5 kg/m² for a maincrop in good conditions. *(The original draft claimed 10–20 kg/m², which is not achievable; corrected.)*
