## 3. Pear (Japanese Pear / Nashi - Pyrus pyrifolia)

### 3.1 Climate Suitability
- **Rating**: ★★★★☆ (High - well-suited to Munakata conditions)
- **Temperature Range**:
  - Growing season: 20-30°C optimal
  - Winter chilling: 200-300 hours below 7°C (Munakata: ~300 hours - sufficient)
  - Heat tolerance: Good; handles 35°C summer days well
- **Rainfall**: 1,500-2,000mm; good, but drainage essential
- **Hardiness Zone**: Zone 7b-9b; Munakata Zone 9b-10a - excellent match

### 3.2 Recommended Varieties for Munakata
| Variety | Characteristics | Harvest Season | Notes |
|---------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| **Hosui** | Explosive sweetness, large, brown skin | October | Most popular; high sugar (13-15 Brix) |
| **Nashi (20th Century)** | Hard flesh, crisp, yellow-green skin | Aug-Sep | Classic; good storage variety |
| **Shinseki** | Strong acidity, aromatic, Sep-Oct | Sep-Oct | Good balance sweet-acid; mid-season |

### 3.3 Planting & Care

**Planting Timeline:**
- **Optimal**: Dormant season Dec-Feb
- **Tree form**: Central leader; requires staking first 3-5 years
- **Pollination**: Most varieties self-unfruitful; need 2+ compatible varieties for best crop

**Planting Procedure:**
1. **Hole**: 60cm diameter × 60cm deep (deeper than peach/peach)
2. **Soil**: 50% native + 30% compost + 20% river sand; pH 6.0-6.5 optimal
3. **Staking**: 1.5m stake for first 3 years; pears grow vigorous upright
4. **Watering**: 40L at planting; consistent moisture first 2-3 years

**Annual Care Calendar:**
| Month | Task |
|-------|------|
| **Dec-Feb** | Planting (dormant), structural pruning, dormant spray |
| **Mar** | Bloom period; monitor for frost (less risk than peach) |
| **Apr** | Fruit set thinning; 1 fruit per 30-40cm branch |
| **May** | Canopy management; scaffold branch training |
| **Jun** | Fruit enlargement; ensure water (drought sensitive) |
| **Jul** | Continue sizing; pest/disease monitoring |
| **Aug-Sep** | **Hosui harvest** (Oct); **Nashi harvest** (Aug-Sep); **Shinseki** (Sep-Oct) |
| **Oct** | Main harvest period complete; post-harvest care |
| **Nov** | Post-harvest fertilizer, soil amendment |

**Fertilization Schedule:**
- **Base** (planting): Organic compost 15-20kg/m² hole
- **Pre-bloom** (Feb): Nitrogen 50g, Phosphorus 80g, Potassium 80g per tree
- **Post-flower** (May): Nitrogen 50g (moderate; too much reduces color)
- **Post-harvest** (Oct-Nov): Potassium 100g, Phosphorus 80g (tree recovery)

### 3.4 Growth Patterns
- **Year 1**: Root establishment; NO fruit harvest
- **Year 2**: Light harvest; 10-20kg per tree if thinned
- **Year 3-4**: Moderate harvest; 20-40kg per tree
- **Year 5+**: Full production; 50-100kg per tree annually
- **Mature tree lifespan**: 30-50 years; pears are long-lived
- **Alternate bearing**: Some varieties produce heavy crop one year, light next; consistent thinning mitigates

### 3.5 Protection & Pest Management

**Common Pests:**
- **Pear psylla**: Sucks sap, produces honeydew; yellow sticky traps, neem oil sprays
- **San Jose scale**: Same as peach; horticultural oil treatments
- **Aphids**: New growth; same controls
- **Codling moth**: Larvae in fruit; pheromone traps, spinosad sprays

**Common Diseases:**
- **Fire blight**: Bacterial; immediately remove infected tissue, copper sprays, antibiotic (if registered)
- **Pear scab**: Fungal; fallen leaf removal, fungicide sprays (captan, chlorothalonil)
- **Brown spot**: Fungal; similar to scab; improved sanitation
- **Powdery mildew**: Fungal; improve air circulation, fungicide if severe
- **Root rot**: Phytophthora; drainage essential, avoid waterlogging

**Protection Methods:**
- **Leaf removal**: Open canopy improves spray penetration & drying
- **Ground cover**: Maintain vegetation to reduce erosion, support beneficial insects
- **Sanitation**: Remove all fallen leaves/fruit mummies before winter
- **Trunk wrapping**: Whitewash防止 sunscald and rodent damage

### 3.6 Harvest & Post-Harvest
- **Harvest indicators**: 
  - Color change (Hosui: brown-yellow; Nashi: yellow-green)
  - Ease of separation (twist-and-lift)
  - Sugar content: 12-15 Brix for Hosui
- **Season**: 
  - Nashi (20th Century): Aug-Sep
  - Hosui: October
  - Shinseki: Sep-Oct
- **Storage**:
  - 3-6 months at 3-7°C, 90-95% humidity
  - Sugar increases during storage (better flavor 1-2 months in storage)
  - Separate by variety; do not store with ethylene-producing fruits
- **Quality**: Best eaten 1-3 months after harvest; flavor improves with storage
